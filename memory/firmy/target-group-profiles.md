# 1. Target Group — 5 Company Profiles for Autonomous CFO

## 1. Pizzeria (5–8 people)
- **Profile:** A small restaurant with a dining room and delivery. The owner handles purchasing, scheduling, and finances themselves in the evening after closing the kitchen.
- **Cash Rhythm:** Daily, small inflows from the cash register/POS terminal and delivery portals (high weekend traffic), mixed with irregular, painful cost spikes: rent, electricity/gas, ingredient purchases (frequent quiet price hikes from wholesalers), and monthly payroll.
- **Main Pain Point:** The illusion of a full cash register on the weekend, followed by a sudden lack of funds for utility bills and social security (ZUS) at the end of the month + eroding margins due to rising cheese/flour prices.
- **What Autonomous CFO Does:** Averages daily revenue by day of the week (Bucket A), watches fixed bills (Bucket B), catches supplier price increases, and suggests simple action cards: *"Delay wholesaler payment by 5 days"* or *"Activate Commerzbank mini-revolving limit for electricity"*.

## 2. Real Estate Developer (100 people — office + construction site)
- **Profile:** A mid-sized company building residential apartment blocks: buys land, finances construction stages, sells apartments, and needs CFO support for the management board.
- **Cash Rhythm:** Extremely "lumpy" (rare, but gigantic transactions): multi-million land purchases and tranches from clients/banks mix with continuous material costs and payroll for 100 people in the office and on the construction site.
- **Main Pain Point:** Timing mismatch between material costs (today) and inflows from the next tranche for sold apartments (in 45 days), plus lack of quick visibility into whether they can afford a new plot without breaking construction liquidity.
- **What Autonomous CFO Does:** Operates on a weekly/milestone timeline, enforces hard limits (Policy-as-Code — no large amount goes through without human approval), forecasts gaps between tranches, and automatically proposes bridge financing (capex / bridge facility) or a money market deposit for temporary surpluses.

## 3. Small Software House (15 people)
- **Profile:** A team of developers building custom applications for B2B clients (Fixed Price or Time & Material projects).
- **Cash Rhythm:** Costs are super predictable (fixed salaries/B2B contracts for 15 people + monthly cloud/SaaS subscriptions), but inflows depend on a few larger invoices for delivered project milestones, which clients often pay late.
- **Main Pain Point:** One delayed transfer from a major client (e.g., +21 days overdue) can wipe out the account right on developer payday. Additionally, unused tool and cloud licenses sit around eating up budget.
- **What Autonomous CFO Does:** Learns client payment habits, warns about payday cash gaps, drafts polite reminders with an early-payment discount, cuts duplicate SaaS subscriptions, and proposes a short Commerzbank credit line as a payroll buffer.

## 4. Transport / Logistics / Freight Forwarding Company (5–30 people)
- **Profile:** A fleet of vans/trucks running domestic and international routes (often invoicing in EUR while incurring costs in PLN/EUR).
- **Cash Rhythm:** Very thin margins and long payment terms (45–60 days from freight forwarders), alongside daily, immediate expenses for fuel, highway tolls, leases, and driver per diems.
- **Main Pain Point:** Killer cash-flow bottlenecks (*"we're running at full capacity, but we don't have money to refuel on Friday"*) and silent losses from EUR/PLN exchange rate fluctuations on foreign invoices.
- **What Autonomous CFO Does:** Combines liquidity forecasting with currency management (Liquidity + FX Hedging): detects a fuel cash shortfall before invoices settle and, with a single swipe, proposes factoring/revolving credit or EUR exchange rate hedging via a Commerzbank product.

## 5. Beauty Salon (4 people)
- **Profile:** An intimate salon (cosmetology/hairdressing) run by an owner who sees clients herself and has neither the time nor the accounting knowledge.
- **Cash Rhythm:** Daily small inflows from appointments (cash/card, higher traffic before holidays/summer), fixed rent for the premises, equipment/laser leasing, cosmetics purchases, and taxes.
- **Main Pain Point:** Financial anxiety (*"I don't understand spreadsheets, I just want to know if I can afford new equipment and if I have enough for ZUS"*) and cash tied up in excessive cosmetics inventory.
- **What Autonomous CFO Does:** Ultra-simple mode (*Innovations for all generations*): zero corporate jargon, friendly messages like talking to a friend (e.g., *"You can safely set aside 4,000 PLN for new equipment, but only after the 15th once rent clears"*), and automatically moving surpluses into a safe deposit.
